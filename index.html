AI Discovery Engine

Evidence-led discovery of customer experience friction and opportunities to improve first-time mutual fund investor SIP retention on Groww.

North-star outcome: Increase the percentage of first-time mutual fund investors who still have an active SIP 12 months after their first SIP. Active SIP: at least one successful instalment in the last 30 days.
Research integrity: All 110 supplied Google Play and Trustpilot reviews are included as valid customer-feedback records about the Groww experience. They are analysed across the full product journey. A review about trading, payments or support is valid app feedback, but it does not automatically prove an SIP-retention problem. Findings below separate observed statements from interpretation and hypotheses. The classifier is a transparent keyword-assisted demo, not a trained AI model.

Product question

What parts of the Groww experience may reduce confidence, create friction or make it harder for a new mutual fund investor to continue investing regularly?

Research questions

  • What problems do customers report across the investment journey?
  • Which problems are directly related to SIPs or mutual funds?
  • What trust, control, transparency or reliability signals appear?
  • Which opportunity is most actionable and testable?

What the dataset can and cannot answer

  • Can: surface reported friction, recurring themes, explicit SIP references and stated exit intentions.
  • Cannot: identify first-time investors, measure 12-month active SIP rate, prove actual SIP cancellation or establish causation.
  • Implication: use review insights to form hypotheses, then validate with SIP user interviews and product analytics.

2. Research method

From raw public feedback to a prioritised product opportunity.

110 raw reviews→Clean & assign IDs→Classify feedback→Extract evidence→Compare problems→Prioritise opportunities→Define experiment
  1. Collection: 110 reviews supplied by the project owner from Google Play Store and Trustpilot.
  2. Preparation: preserve review text, date, source and URL; assign stable IDs GR001–GR110.
  3. Classification: apply keyword-assisted labels for product area, feedback theme, experience friction and explicit SIP/mutual-fund context.
  4. Evidence extraction: retain the original wording and distinguish what the reviewer explicitly says from what the product team interprets.
  5. Synthesis: group feedback into problem themes and map each proposed opportunity to supporting review IDs.
  6. Prioritisation: assess user impact, evidence strength, potential reach and ability to test—not sentiment score alone.

The current prototype uses deterministic keyword rules and human-readable labels. It does not call an external AI service or train a machine-learning model.

Research interpretation

Public reviews point to possible friction around control, transparency, transaction reliability and support. For recurring investments, these areas may matter because an SIP depends on predictable scheduling, successful payment execution and confidence in how money is invested and redeemed. The current reviews do not establish that these issues caused users to stop SIPs; this relationship must be tested directly.

6. Opportunity areas

Each opportunity links to the user evidence and a validation question.

Priority 1 · Closest to SIP journey

Make SIP controls predictable

Show the exact skip/pause eligibility window, cut-off time, next debit date and a clear success/failure confirmation. Explain what action is still possible if the cut-off has passed.

Evidence: explicit failed SIP skip attempt (search “skip my sips” in the evidence table).

Validate: Can users correctly understand when and how they can pause or skip an instalment? How often do attempts fail?

Priority 2 · Transparency

Make plan choice and charges easier to understand

Explain direct vs regular plan, applicable commission/expense differences and whether a selected plan can be changed before confirmation. Make correction paths clear.

Evidence: mutual-fund review reporting additional commission and intent to redeem/move platforms.

Validate: Do first-time investors understand the plan they selected and the costs that apply?

Priority 3 · Reliability

Improve payment and transaction status visibility

Provide clear payment status, retry guidance, mandate status and resolution steps when money is debited but an investment or switch is pending.

Evidence: reviews reporting payment errors, blocked/debited funds and fund-switch status issues.

Validate: What proportion of SIP instalments fail, remain pending or need support intervention?

Priority 4 · Trust & service

Set transparent redemption expectations

Show expected processing stages, status updates, likely timelines and a clear escalation route for mutual-fund redemption.

Evidence: reviews describing delayed withdrawal/redemption and poor support experiences.

Validate: Does uncertainty about access to invested money reduce confidence or lead to platform switching?

Supporting evidence

Opportunity areas are hypotheses derived from the supplied reviews and require validation with first-time SIP investors and product data.

7. AI-assisted review classifier

Interactive demonstration of how a review can be tagged and routed for product discovery. This prototype uses transparent keyword rules; it is not a trained AI model.

Try the classifier

Classifier output fields

  • Product area: SIP / mutual funds, payments, redemption, support, app reliability, trading or other.
  • Feedback theme: control, transparency, transaction failure, delay, data accuracy, support, usability.
  • Experience signal: issue, positive feedback, comparison, or stated exit intention.
  • SIP relevance: explicit SIP wording, mutual-fund context, or broader Groww experience.
  • Evidence confidence: based on explicit wording; uncertain context is flagged for human review.

How the classifier should be evaluated

Manually label a representative sample, compare classifier tags against human labels, and calculate precision, recall and confusion matrix by category. Review false positives (e.g., stock brokerage charges incorrectly treated as mutual-fund charges) and false negatives (SIP wording missed due to spelling or synonyms). Do not use sentiment as a substitute for problem classification.

8. Selected solution hypothesis

Choose the first opportunity because it is closest to the recurring SIP journey and has an explicit review describing a failed control action.

Customer review→SIP schedule control friction→Predictable SIP controls→In-context schedule & status panel

Problem

A reviewer says the app indicates an SIP can be skipped within a stated time window, but the skip action does not work even before that window closes.

Solution hypothesis

If Groww clearly shows SIP skip eligibility, cut-off time, next debit date and an immediate action status, users will better understand their options and experience fewer failed or uncertain schedule changes.

MVP concept

  • Upcoming SIP card with next debit date and amount.
  • Skip/pause eligibility and cut-off countdown (only when supported by actual policy).
  • Confirmation screen with reference/status.
  • Failure reason and next best action.

9. Evaluation plan & success metrics

Measure whether the proposed intervention improves understanding and actual SIP continuity without encouraging unsuitable investing.

Discovery validation

  • Interview new SIP investors who successfully continued, paused, missed or stopped an instalment.
  • Ask what happened immediately before the missed/paused SIP and what they expected the app to do.
  • Review anonymised SIP mandate, debit, retry, skip/pause and support-contact funnel data.
  • Segment by first SIP age, tenure, payment method and issue type only where consented and available.

Prototype usability test

  • Give participants a task: find the next SIP date and explain whether they can skip an instalment.
  • Measure task completion, time on task, correct understanding and confidence rating.
  • Observe whether users can find the reason and next action after a simulated failure.
  • Compare current journey with the proposed clear-status journey.

10. Assignment questions — concise answers

What is the key customer problem?

Public feedback suggests that customers may experience friction around recurring-investment control, plan/charge clarity, payment reliability and access to support. The clearest direct SIP example is a failed attempt to skip an instalment. These are discovery signals, not proven causes of 12-month churn.

What evidence supports it?

110 original reviews across Google Play and Trustpilot, including an explicit SIP skip complaint, mutual-fund plan/commission concerns with stated exit intention, and broader reports of payment, transaction, redemption and support issues.

What opportunity should be prioritised?

Make SIP schedule controls predictable and transparent, then validate the scale of failed actions and their relationship to missed instalments or cancellations.

What is the proposed solution?

An in-context SIP schedule and action-status experience showing next debit, eligibility, cut-off, confirmation and failure recovery.

How will success be measured?

Use the 12-month active-SIP north-star metric, supported by SIP action success, instalment success, failed-action support rate and user comprehension metrics.

What is still unknown?

Whether reviewers are first-time investors, whether their SIPs were discontinued, how common these issues are among SIP users, and whether fixing them changes 12-month retention. These require interviews, analytics and controlled testing.
